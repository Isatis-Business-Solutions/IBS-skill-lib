# IBS stock images

An IBS image **makes the hook of the content visible**, so that someone scrolling past stops and wants to
read. It is a realistic, **contemporary and modern** photograph in **golden-hour light**, with **nature** in
the scene and **one object from yesterday's future**. It stirs an emotion and stays professional.

## The image, defined

- **Message first** — the image carries the core or the hook of the content it goes with. Someone who has not
  read the text still senses what it is about and wants to know more. It hints; it does not give everything
  away, and it is never clickbait. Value comes before entertainment, but an image is never without
  entertainment.
- **The angle** — the image may show the angle of the message exaggerated: one bold, slightly surreal
  intervention in an otherwise realistic photo that pushes the point further than reality would. One at most;
  everything else in the scene stays believable.
- **Setting** — when the content is about a use case or example in a sector, the image is set in that sector:
  the places and objects people working there recognise as their own, in their modern form. When the content
  names no sector, choose the world of the reader of the content and say which you chose.
- **People** — welcome, as many as the story needs, never required. Present-day people in modern clothing,
  natural rather than posed. They look at the world, at each other, at the camera, or interact with the
  object from yesterday's future; they are not shown simply looking at a screen.
- **Technology** — supports the story and never takes over the image. The interaction between people and
  machines is shown first of all through the object from yesterday's future, which may feel a little magical.
  Ordinary devices may appear when the story needs them, never by default. No data visuals, dashboards or
  readable screen content.
- **Contemporary modern** — the base of every image. Places, people and things look like today at its most
  current. Never manual craft or workshop scenes, never old, industrial-looking or conservative settings,
  never vintage props, unless the content is explicitly about them.
- **Realistic** — photographed with the quality of a good editorial stock photo. No illustration, no
  3D-render look. The bold intervention (see *The angle*) is the only departure from realism.
- **Golden hour** — low sun near the horizon, long soft shadows, warm yellow-to-orange light. This light sets
  the colour of the whole image.
- **Nature** — a living landscape is part of the scene: outdoors, seen through windows, or growing in the
  space itself; never mere decoration. Nature is shown by what is in the image, never by colour: no green
  tint or green grading; under golden-hour light greens turn warm.
- **Yesterday's future** — exactly one object that looks like the future as the late 1980s and the 1990s
  imagined it, up to the Y2K era: glossy, rounded, blobby, translucent, chrome or iridescent, more future than
  present. Preferably it takes part in the story (the machine the people interact with); it fits the topic.
  Everything around it stays today's modern world.
- **Emotion with professionalism** — the image stirs an emotion (recognition, surprise, a smile) and stays
  professional: one strong idea, an uncluttered background, warm and well made, never loud. The subject is
  large enough to read at thumbnail size in a feed.
- **No text in the image** — no text, letters, logos or watermarks. Headlines and labels are set in the
  layout around the image, so the image needs no empty area reserved for them. The aspect ratio follows the
  layout it goes into.

## How to work

1. Read the content: what is the hook, what is the angle, and what does the reader gain from reading on?
2. Choose the setting: the sector of the use case, or the world of the reader.
3. Choose one scene. Decide who is in it (if anyone), whether one bold intervention makes the angle visible,
   and what the object from yesterday's future is and how it takes part.
4. Write the image prompt with every element above, plus what to avoid. Write it in English.
5. Make the image with the `image-generator` skill if it is available; it asks the user before it ever falls
   back to a prompt. If that skill is not installed, ask the user whether they want the prompt and a
   placeholder, and only then give them.
6. Check the result against the list below; if one point clearly fails, regenerate with that point fixed.

## Check

- Without the text, does someone sense what the content is about, and want to read it?
- Value before entertainment, but not without it; an emotion, still professional; no clickbait?
- When there is a sector: would someone from it recognise their own world?
- Golden-hour light, warm colour, no green cast? Nature visible as part of the scene?
- Contemporary modern: no craft, workshop, industrial or old-fashioned look?
- Exactly one object from yesterday's future (late 1980s–1990s, Y2K), ideally part of the story?
- Technology supporting, not dominating; nobody simply staring at a screen?
- Realistic, with at most one bold intervention?
- No text, logos, data visuals or readable screens?
- One strong idea, readable at thumbnail size?

## Not for

Infographics, diagrams, charts, icons and other explanatory visuals. Build those in the medium itself,
following `ibs-huisstijl` if it is available.
