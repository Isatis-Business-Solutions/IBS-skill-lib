# The brief: grill until it is clear

A clip edited on a vague story cannot be fixed later; every later step only executes the brief. So the brief
comes first, and it is finished only when every field below is filled, concrete, and confirmed by the user.

## Fields (`brief` in production.json)

| Field | Holds | Clear when |
|---|---|---|
| `product` | what is offered, in one sentence: what it does and how | a stranger could explain it back |
| `buyer` | who decides to buy or act on the clip | a role, not "companies" |
| `user` | who uses the product day to day, if not the buyer | a role and their situation |
| `goal` | "After this clip [who] must [think, feel or do] …" | names the viewer and one outcome |
| `core` | the one thing the viewer must remember | one sentence, no "and" |
| `channel`, `format`, `length_s` | where it runs, aspect, target length | given or decided |
| `cta` | what the viewer does next, and where | a concrete action (or "none", deliberately) |
| `footage` | what real footage exists | a list, or "none" |

Optional when they matter: `proof` (facts, numbers with their source), `mood`, `avoid`.

## How to grill

- One question at a time, the most consequential open field first. Offer a recommended answer with each
  question; the user corrects faster than they invent.
- Before asking, check what the request, earlier messages and the project files already answer.
- Push past the first answer when it is vague ("our clients", "it saves time"): ask who exactly, how,
  compared to what.
- Ask how the product is used, step by step, from the user's point of view. Those steps become the
  explaining beats and graphics later.
- Never invent facts, names or numbers; a missing fact becomes a placeholder in `brief.proof` or an open
  question.
- End by reading the brief back in five lines and asking for a yes.

Use the `grilling` skill for the interview when it is installed.
